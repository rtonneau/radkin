# IRT stage: own particle solver with Geant4's sampling formulas

_ADR 0007, recorded 2026-11-15 (session 2026-11-15__irt-syn-port)._

<!-- Format adapted from mattpocock-skills' domain-modeling (ADR-FORMAT.md): https://github.com/mattpocock/skills.git -->

radkin took its molecules from Geant4-DNA at a handover time chosen in the Geant4 run, so the particle chemistry before it was Geant4's IRT_syn: one track at a time, without background reactions, and six Geant4 passes per study. The RDME cannot replace it from 1 ps by refining the grid (PR #18: •OH +40 % at 10 us). radkin now has its own IRT stage (`src/irt/`), from the 1 ps molecules to a handover time or to the end of the run, so that Geant4 is only needed up to the end of the physico-chemical stage.

Decisions:

- **Own solver, Geant4's formulas.** The reaction time of a pair, the contact probability and the reaction and product positions are those of Geant4-DNA 11.4.3 (`G4DiffusionControlledReactionModel`, `G4DNAMakeReaction`, `G4DNAMolecularReactionTable`). The event loop is radkin's, built for many overlapping tracks. The Geant4 match is the acceptance test, not the design constraint: two places where Geant4's code departs from the formulas it implements are not copied (the standard deviation of the pair's diffusion, the axis of the angle at the reaction).
- **Two resampling rules, incremental by default.** Incremental resampling (times kept, only the pairs of new molecules drawn) is the default and the reference: on 2000 single-track events in free space it is within 1 % of Geant4 for every main species from 100 ps to 10 us (H2O2 +1.5 %), and it is 600 times faster than the full rule at 100 overlapping tracks. It is also what Geant4's IRT_syn does, which keeps its reaction set between reaction steps. Full resampling (every reaction time drawn again after each reaction) was designed as the reference and was the default of the first version; it reacts a spur slightly too fast (H2O2 +5.5 %, •OH -1.5 %, e_aq +1.5 % at 10 us), because each pass draws a pair again as if no depletion had built up around it. It stays as a cross-check. The 100-event reference of the first acceptance test could not tell the two rules apart.
- **Reaction type.** The type a network gives (`irt_type`, written by the Geant4 export) wins. Without one, a reaction is fully diffusion-controlled when `k_obs >= k_dif` (the diffusion limit of the van der Waals radii, Onsager-corrected for ions) and partially otherwise, with no free parameter. On Geant4's 52 pair reactions the rule gives Geant4's type for 47; the other 5 are reactions Geant4 types fully diffusion-controlled below that limit. A species missing from the species table makes its reactions fully diffusion-controlled, with a warning.
- **Background.** A species with an initial concentration is a background species: a particle reacts with it at the rate `k c`, as a reaction of one particle with an exponential time. The background keeps its concentration; what it loses is counted, not debited.
- **Walls.** Reflective walls fold positions back into the box, and a pair near a wall also samples a reaction time against the mirror image of its partner (first image across each wall, with the edge and corner images). The image is one more independent pair. This is right when the wall is far compared with the reaction radius (checked against a Brownian walk 7 radii from a wall) and wrong in a box of about 10 radii, where a sink and its image compete: the rule then reacts a pair 1.5 times too fast.
- **One handover time.** `irt.t_end` is the end of the stage and, in `radkin voxel` with the ssa solver, the time at which its molecules are binned onto the grid for the RDME. A seed at or after it is an error.

Consequences and limits:

- IRT treats pairs as independent. This gets worse as tracks overlap densely, and nothing here measures that error: multi-track aerated results stay provisional until a Geant4 multi-track reference exists (idea `geant4-uhdr-multi-track-reference`).
- The first Geant4 reference (a 2 um water box in vacuum) is not free space after 300 ns: it loses molecules at the faces of its box. `boundary = "outflow"` reproduces that loss; it is not an absorbing wall and has no other use. The second reference puts 5 um of water around the same segment and writes its seeds in the G(t) pass itself, which gives the free-space comparison above.
- A pair that a move brings inside its effective radius gets no reaction time until it has moved apart again (as in Geant4), with the next step bounded by `r_eff^2 / D`. Reacting it at once disagrees with Geant4 at 10 ps.
- Not done: anything before 1 ps (dissociation channels, electron thermalisation), several handover times, a handover to the deterministic voxel solver.
