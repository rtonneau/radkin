How the RDME papers in docs/literature/md_mistral can benefit radkin

Direction: read the _lite.md versions of the five papers and map them onto radkin's stochastic voxel path (src/ssa/rdme*.rs, ADR 0003, docs/ssa.md). radkin already cites Hellander 2012 for its h* warning (RdmeSetup::validity_bound); the papers go further.

1. Mesh-dependent bimolecular propensities (Strong)

Papers: Hellander, Hellander, Petzold 2012 (PRE 85, 042901); Hellander & Hellander 2020 (JCP 152, 034104), eq. 6-8.

Finding. radkin's RDME uses the macroscopic rate constant unchanged in every voxel (k/(N_A V)). Between h* and h_min this underestimates bimolecular encounters: the molecules first have to diffuse into the same voxel. The 3D correction of eq. 6-7 is 1/(k_meso h^3) = 1/k_r + 1/(4 pi sigma D) - C3/(6 h D), C3 = 1.5164. With the macroscopic constant 1/k = 1/k_r + 1/(4 pi sigma D) this becomes k_eff(h) = k / (1 - C3 k / (6 h D)) = k / (1 - h*/h), with no reaction radius needed. Its pole is exactly the h* = 0.2527 k/(N_A D) that radkin already computes. At the 6 nm voxels of data/voxel_example.toml with h* = 1-4 nm for the radical recombinations, the factor is 1.2x-3x, which is large enough to change the small-voxel G-values table in docs/ssa.md.

Open points. Reactions with h <= h* (e.g. e_aq + O_minus + H2O, h* = 75 nm) have no local correction; keep them uncorrected and warn, or refuse. Pseudo-bimolecular reactions written with H2O. 2 A reactions (D = 2 D_A). Whether the coarsening levels use the correction with their own h. Validation: the mean association time of one A and one B (Hellander 2012 eq. 1 and Theorem 1: N_steps = 1.5164 N) against V/k.

2. Per-species grid resolution: multi-grid RDME (Worth exploring)

Papers: Erban & Winkelmann 2024 (Bull. Math. Biol. 87:6, mgRDME); Hellander & Hellander 2020 (hRDME).

Finding. The cost of radkin's RDME is mostly hops, 6 D/h^2 per molecule (docs/ssa.md, "Cost"). Today coarsening is global: the whole grid is at one level. The mgRDME gives each species its own fixed grid of the 2^L hierarchy. Fast or well-mixed species (O2, H3O+, OH-, H2O2 later on) live on a coarse level and seeded radicals on the fine one. The coupling rules are simple and fit radkin's nested Cartesian levels. A bimolecular reaction between a fine and a coarse molecule uses the rate of the coarser voxel (hRDME III.C.4). A product made on a coarser grid than its own goes uniformly to one of the children (mgRDME eq. 20 and 25). The mgRDME results: the error on the fine species barely grows while the cost falls, the Pareto front is all multi-grid, gamma ~ sqrt(D_A/D_B) balances the hop rates, and for a second-order reaction an optimal coarse grid exists, because a coarser grid also keeps the reactions that very small voxels lose. hRDME eq. 13-14 pick the coarsest admissible level per species from a tolerance W(h) = (k/D) G(h, sigma) < eps.

Open points. It pays only where a species is numerous and fast. That is the case for aerated runs or voxels of 100 nm and up (O2 at 2.5e-4 M is about 150 molecules in a 100 nm voxel), not for the 6 nm track grids, where radicals dominate. Output: coarse species are spread over the fine voxels, as today. Interaction with global coarsening and tau-leaping. Deterministic path: ADI per species on its own grid is possible but its cost is in the reaction step.

3. Per-molecule mesh transfer: hRDME (Speculative)

Paper: Hellander & Hellander 2020.

Finding. Products of a dissociation (or the molecules of a spur) start on the finest mesh. They move one level up once they have diffused for t_transfer = C h^2 / (6D) (C = 1 to 20), and reach the well-mixed level when they survive. The reported speed-ups are 400-2000x over eGFRD and over the fully resolved RDME at the same accuracy. In radkin's terms this is a local, per-species, age-driven coarsening, as opposed to the global dispersion test of rdme_coarsen.rs. It fits a radiolysis track well: spurs are dense at t0 and then dilute. It needs a next-particle method (per-molecule ages), while radkin uses the count-based next-subvolume method. An approximation by age cohorts per species and voxel would be needed, which is research.

4. Convergent RDME: reactions between neighbouring voxels (Speculative)

Paper: Isaacson & Zhang 2018 (JCP, unstructured-mesh CRDME for reversible reactions).

Finding. The CRDME discretises the Doi model. A in V_i and B in V_j react with kappa_ij = lambda |{(x,y) in V_i x V_j : |x-y| < eps}| / (|V_i||V_j|), and the product is placed by kappa_k|ij. Below h* it converges instead of losing reactions. It is identical to the RDME for hops and first-order reactions. On radkin's Cartesian grid the overlap integrals depend only on the voxel offset, so they form a small precomputed stencil. This is the only path that makes nm-scale voxels, and the 75 nm h* reaction, valid. Cost: a Doi radius and rate per reaction (from k, D and a radius convention), propensities summed over neighbour pairs, and a rework of the event bookkeeping. Large.

5. CDME (del Razo et al. 2023): framework, little direct use (Speculative)

Finding. A formal particle-level master equation (a Fock-space family of Fokker-Planck equations) with the exact combinatorial factors for decay, binding and annihilation. It suggests no algorithm for radkin. What it can do is document, in docs/ssa.md, the derivation that the 2 A propensity k n(n-1)/(N_A V) and the hop and reaction rules are the well-mixed and lattice limits of one model. The factor 2 is already validated numerically (docs/ssa.md). Documentation only.

Not taken

  • Unstructured meshes (2018, 2024 discussion): radkin's geometry is a uniform Cartesian box and no non-box geometry is planned.
